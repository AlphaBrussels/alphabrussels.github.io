Title
Stocks rallied. Nvidia didn’t.
Subtitle
US stocks had their best week since August and Europe hit record highs, even as Nvidia lost its founder-CEO and bitcoin broke below $70,000.

The week’s moves

*The DAX reinvests dividends, which flatters its gain next to the other indices. [1] The STOXX 600 and BEL 20 closed at record highs. [2][3] Asia’s gains are a rebound from the previous Friday’s tech rout, when the Nikkei and Kospi each fell about 6%. [4]

Energy was the best-performing S&P 500 sector (+5.4%), and all eleven sectors finished higher. [5]

Nvidia’s week, day by day

Down 12.7% to $162.85, a sixth straight weekly fall and the longest losing streak since 2012. [6][7]

  • Mon 9 (−4.3%) A board probe of Jensen Huang, announced on Sunday, hits the stock. [8][7]
  • Tue 10 (−5.5%) More selling on the day of the hot US inflation print. [7]
  • Wed 11 (+2.9%) Shares recover some ground. [7]
  • Thu 12 (−4.3%) Huang resigns and CFO Colette Kress becomes interim CEO. The stock touches $148.80, about 14% down, before recovering. [9][7]
  • Fri 13 (−2.0%) A Truist downgrade; at least ten analysts have cut to Hold. Market value closes below $4 trillion. [10][11]

In Asia, SoftBank fell 5.1% and SK Hynix 3% on Friday, while Taiwan’s Taiex rose and TSMC edged up. [12] Nvidia reports earnings on Tuesday after the US close. [13]

Why prices moved

A rebound from a rough patch. Monday’s +2.4% S&P 500 bounce followed a sell-off on 5–6 November, and the VIX fell 22% in a single session. [14] Falling oil helped: crude drifted lower for a second week as the US blockade of Iran eased fears of renewed fighting. [15]

Hot inflation, higher yields. October CPI rose 0.4% (core 0.3%, a third straight month), and wholesale prices ran 6.5% above last year. [16] Fed Governor Waller said a core print near 0.3% could put a 50 bp December hike on the table, and the 10-year yield climbed every session. [14] Traders priced roughly an 89% chance of a 25 bp December hike, per CME FedWatch on Thursday. [17] That weighed on small caps and real estate. [18]

Nvidia loses its CEO. The board opened a probe into Jensen Huang on 8 November, and he resigned Thursday after reports of extramarital affairs. CFO Colette Kress is interim CEO. [9] The stock fell as much as 14% intraday, closed Thursday down 4.3%, and ended Friday below a $4 trillion market value for the first time since July 2025. [6] Money rotated to rivals: AMD drew an Oppenheimer upgrade, and Broadcom benefited from cloud giants building their own AI chips. [19]

Europe shrugged off the chip rout. The STOXX 600 gained 2.4% to a record, helped by strong bank earnings and industrial orders. [18] Insurers, oil majors and names like Burberry and Munich Re offset the chip sell-off, [2] and European equity funds took in $35 billion in the week to 11 November. [18] Brussels’ defensive BEL 20 notched its 34th record close of the year on Friday, led by KBC. [3]

Crypto took the hit. Bitcoin fell through its August low below $68,000 on Friday, its weakest since mid-August, as rate-hike bets, forced selling (over $1.3 billion liquidated) and Nvidia’s turmoil collided. [20][21] Spot bitcoin ETFs had logged five straight sessions of outflows (about $1.44 billion) through 11 November. [22]

Other news that mattered

  • Midterms reshape the policy outlook. Democrats won both chambers of Congress; Elizabeth Warren is set to chair Senate Banking, which could change the path of US crypto legislation in 2027. [23] Analysts also flag possible data-center regulation. [24]
  • Huang summoned to testify. Lawmakers have called on the former Nvidia chief to appear before Congress. [25]
  • Alphabet widens its AI-chip push. It announced a broader range of AI chips this week, positioning itself as a challenger to Nvidia. [26]
  • Euro-area growth holds steady. GDP grew 0.4% on the quarter (1.3% on the year), but employment was flat. [18]
  • Middle East still a supply risk. Shipping attacks continue even as oil eases, and the Strait of Hormuz remains the key chokepoint. [15]

What to expect next week

  • MON Fed Chair Kevin Warsh speaks. [24]
  • TUE October US retail sales and UK jobs data; Home Depot and Disney report; Nvidia reports after the close, its first quarter since Huang’s exit. Consensus is roughly $109 billion in revenue. [16][13]
  • WED FOMC minutes (2 pm ET) show how many officials leaned toward a hike; Target and Lowe’s report; UK inflation (CPI); Trump–Xi meet at APEC (18–19 Nov). [16][27]
  • THU Walmart and Alibaba report. Anthropic is reported to be targeting a Nasdaq debut. [27]
  • FRI Final University of Michigan consumer sentiment; EU–UK summit. Euro-area industrial output is also due (forecast +0.6%). [18][27]

Levels to watch. One outlook’s base case has the S&P 500 ranging between 7,450 and 7,700, with a break above 7,700 turning the picture bullish and a drop below 7,450 bearish. [18] Bitcoin’s next big support is near $67,000, the 200-week moving average. [28]
