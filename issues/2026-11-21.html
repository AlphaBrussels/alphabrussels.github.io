Do the maths: the S&P 500’s week

Where does the −8.6% in the table come from? You only need two closing prices.1

Step 1Write down last Friday’s close, on 13 November.17,561.67
Step 2Write down this Friday’s close, on 20 November.16,910.39
Step 3Subtract the first from the second: the index lost 651.28 points over the week.40−651.28
Step 4Divide that loss by last week’s close and multiply by a hundred to turn it into a percentage.1,40−8.6%

Your turn: the BEL 20 went from 6,119.66 to 5,797.75 this week. Do the same four steps and you should get −5.3%.10

In plain words
Why percentages, not points? A fall of a few hundred points means a lot for a small index and very little for a big one. Turning every move into a percentage puts all markets on the same ruler, which is why the table can compare a Japanese index, a Belgian index and bitcoin side by side.
 
Why prices moved

Worries about AI spread. The trade in artificial intelligence (AI) shares has been losing steam partly over worries that AI stocks’ big market values and heavy spending are unjustified.41 On Wednesday the European Central Bank (ECB) warned that ambitious profit hopes for AI firms leave financial markets vulnerable to abrupt adjustments.42 By Thursday the S&P 500 had fallen for six days in a row, its longest losing streak in two and a half years.43 Over the week the S&P 500 fell 8.6% and the Nasdaq Composite, full of tech companies, 10%, leaving the Nasdaq lower than at the start of the year.40 Friday alone took the S&P 500 down 3.7%, the Dow Jones 1.9% and the Nasdaq 5.3%.19

In plain words
What is a correction? When an index falls more than a tenth below its latest record high, professional investors call it a correction. It is a sign that the mood has turned, but not that a crash is coming: markets have gone through many corrections and later recovered. A fall of more than a fifth is called a bear market.

Nvidia beat forecasts and still fell. In its first results since boss Jensen Huang stepped down as chief executive, the chipmaker reported sales of $115.84 billion after the market closed on Tuesday, above the $109.11 billion analysts expected.44 It also forecast about $130 billion for the next three months, against the $125.3 billion analysts had pencilled in.45 The shares rose 4% in late trading, but the jump faded on Wednesday.44,46 Reuters reported that even a strong forecast did not clear away the uncertainty hanging over the company.47 On Friday Reuters reported that the SEC, the US stock market regulator, is looking into Nvidia’s accounting; the shares were already down more than 6% before that report and closed 13.8% lower on the day.41,48 Other chipmakers were pulled down too: on Friday alone AMD lost 13.25% and Broadcom 12.19%.29,30

In plain words
What does a regulator’s investigation mean? The SEC is the US agency that polices stock markets and checks that companies report their numbers honestly. An investigation does not mean a company did anything wrong. But it creates uncertainty: investors cannot be sure the figures they rely on will hold, or whether fines may follow. When investors are unsure, many sell first and wait for answers later.

US interest rates may go up. Notes from the last meeting of the Federal Reserve (the Fed, the US central bank), published on Wednesday, discussed further action to raise its key interest rate, and a couple of officials said a rise of more than 25 basis points (a basis point is one hundredth of a percentage point) could be considered at the next meeting if inflation data were unfavorable.49 Traders saw a 90% chance of a 25 basis point rise in December.50 That matters for AI shares because investors worry that high interest rates would mean less borrowed money to fund the industry’s growth.43

In plain words
A chain reaction, step by step. Worrying inflation news → the Fed expects to raise interest rates → borrowing gets more expensive and safe savings pay more → companies that borrow to grow, and whose big profits are years away, look less attractive → their shares fall. Each arrow is one reason, and a surprise at the start can ripple all the way to the end. Learning to follow chains like this is how investors read the news.

Gold lost its shine. Gold futures (contracts to buy gold at a later date) lost 9.5% over the week, and on Wednesday the price of gold for immediate delivery hit its lowest level since September 2025.34,38 Reuters gave the reason: higher interest rates raise the opportunity cost of holding gold, which pays no interest, so it looks less attractive to investors.38

In plain words
What is an opportunity cost? It is what you give up by choosing one thing over another. Money kept in gold earns nothing while you hold it; money kept in a savings account or a government bond earns interest. When interest rates rise, the interest you give up by holding gold gets bigger, so some investors swap gold for assets that pay.

Europe and Asia followed Wall Street. The STOXX 600 fell 5.8%, its steepest weekly decline since late March 2025, and on Friday chip-linked stocks such as ASML and STMicroelectronics shed more than 9%.5,21,51 Deutsche Bank fell 6% on Tuesday after media reports based on leaked documents detailed the bank’s dealings.52 In Brussels the BEL 20 lost more than 100 points on Tuesday alone, closing at 5,986.98.53 On Friday the bank KBC fell 3.35%, and AB InBev was the only BEL 20 share to rise.25 In Asia, Taiwan’s market slid as investors unwound AI-related bets over concerns about valuations (how highly shares are priced), profitability and rising debt, and Japan’s Nikkei had its lowest close since April 13.32,54 From Monday, Taiwan will fully ban short-selling below the previous day’s closing price.32

In plain words
What is short-selling? A short-seller borrows shares, sells them, and hopes to buy them back later at a lower price before returning them. The difference is their profit. Short-selling lets investors bet on falling prices, but in a fast fall it can add extra selling pressure, which is why some markets limit it during a rout.

Crypto fell with tech. Bitcoin lost 13.8% over the week.15 Reuters linked the slide in crypto markets to concern about the profits from massive AI spending, rising borrowing costs and regulatory risks following Democrats’ win in the US midterm elections.55

 
Other news that mattered
 
Anthropic’s record listing. The AI company priced its shares at $83 and raised $81.3 billion, the largest stock market debut on record. The shares opened at $111.50 on Thursday, then fell 9.1% on Friday.56–59
 
Walmart beat forecasts and barely moved. Its profit per share was 65 cents, just above the 64 cents analysts expected, and the shares fell a little less than 1% in afternoon trading.60
 
US shoppers kept spending. Retail sales, what Americans spend in shops, rose 0.2% in October, in line with what economists expected.61
 
UK inflation ticked up. Prices in the UK were 3.6% higher than a year earlier in October, up from 3.5% the month before and in line with forecasts.62,63
 
Japan’s economy shrank slightly. It contracted at an annualized rate (the pace over a full year if it kept going) of 0.1% in July to September, hurt by surging oil prices because of the war in Iran.64
In plain words
What is an IPO? An initial public offering (IPO) is when a company sells its shares on a stock exchange for the first time. Before it, the company is owned by its founders, staff and early investors; after it, anyone can buy a piece. The price is set the day before trading starts, and the first trades show how much demand there really is.

In plain words
Test yourself. Three questions on this issue’s ideas, answers at the end. One: a company’s results beat what analysts expected, yet its shares fall. How can that happen? Two: why can gold lose value when interest rates go up? Three: an index ends a little more than a tenth below its record high. What do investors call that? Answers. One: prices react to how results compare with what investors hoped for, so a beat that is smaller than hoped, or other bad news, can still push shares down. Two: gold pays no interest, so holding it means missing out on more interest. Three: a correction.
