## Why prices moved

**Worries about AI spread.** The trade in artificial intelligence (AI) shares has been losing steam partly over worries that AI stocks’ big market values and heavy spending are unjustified.[@ap-fri-correction] On Wednesday the European Central Bank (ECB) warned that ambitious profit hopes for AI firms leave financial markets vulnerable to abrupt adjustments.[@ecb-fsr] By Thursday the S&P 500 had fallen for six days in a row, its longest losing streak in two and a half years.[@ap-thu-longest-streak] Over the week the S&P 500 fell 8.6% and the Nasdaq Composite, full of tech companies, 10%, leaving the Nasdaq lower than at the start of the year.[@ap-us-indexes-week] Friday alone took the S&P 500 down 3.7%, the Dow Jones 1.9% and the Nasdaq 5.3%.[@ap-us-indexes]

explain: **What is a correction?** When an index falls more than a tenth below its latest record high, professional investors call it a correction. It is a sign that the mood has turned, but not that a crash is coming: markets have gone through many corrections and later recovered. A fall of more than a fifth is called a bear market.

**Nvidia beat forecasts and still fell.** In its first results since boss Jensen Huang stepped down as chief executive, the chipmaker reported sales of $115.84 billion after the market closed on Tuesday, above the $109.11 billion analysts expected.[@reuters-nvidia-results] It also forecast about $130 billion for the next three months, against the $125.3 billion analysts had pencilled in.[@bloomberg-nvidia-outlook] The shares rose 4% in late trading, but the jump faded on Wednesday.[@reuters-nvidia-results][@ap-wed-fifth-drop] Reuters reported that even a strong forecast did not clear away the uncertainty hanging over the company.[@reuters-nvidia-retreats] On Friday Reuters reported that the SEC, the US stock market regulator, is looking into Nvidia’s accounting; the shares were already down more than 6% before that report and closed 13.8% lower on the day.[@reuters-nvidia-sec][@ap-fri-correction] Other chipmakers were pulled down too: on Friday alone AMD lost 13.25% and Broadcom 12.19%.[@stockanalysis-amd][@stockanalysis-avgo]

explain: **What does a regulator’s investigation mean?** The SEC is the US agency that polices stock markets and checks that companies report their numbers honestly. An investigation does not mean a company did anything wrong. But it creates uncertainty: investors cannot be sure the figures they rely on will hold, or whether fines may follow. When investors are unsure, many sell first and wait for answers later.

**US interest rates may go up.** Notes from the last meeting of the Federal Reserve (the Fed, the US central bank), published on Wednesday, discussed further action to raise its key interest rate, and a couple of officials said a rise of more than 25 basis points (a basis point is one hundredth of a percentage point) could be considered at the next meeting if inflation data were unfavorable.[@fed-fomc-minutes] Traders saw a 90% chance of a 25 basis point rise in December.[@reuters-gold-thu] That matters for AI shares because investors worry that high interest rates would mean less borrowed money to fund the industry’s growth.[@ap-thu-longest-streak]

explain: **A chain reaction, step by step.** Worrying inflation news → the Fed expects to raise interest rates → borrowing gets more expensive and safe savings pay more → companies that borrow to grow, and whose big profits are years away, look less attractive → their shares fall. Each arrow is one reason, and a surprise at the start can ripple all the way to the end. Learning to follow chains like this is how investors read the news.

**Gold lost its shine.** Gold futures (contracts to buy gold at a later date) lost 9.5% over the week, and on Wednesday the price of gold for immediate delivery hit its lowest level since September 2025.[@yahoo-gc][@reuters-gold-wed] Reuters gave the reason: higher interest rates raise the opportunity cost of holding gold, which pays no interest, so it looks less attractive to investors.[@reuters-gold-wed]

explain: **What is an opportunity cost?** It is what you give up by choosing one thing over another. Money kept in gold earns nothing while you hold it; money kept in a savings account or a government bond earns interest. When interest rates rise, the interest you give up by holding gold gets bigger, so some investors swap gold for assets that pay.

**Europe and Asia followed Wall Street.** The STOXX 600 fell 5.8%, its steepest weekly decline since late March 2025, and on Friday chip-linked stocks such as ASML and STMicroelectronics shed more than 9%.[@yahoo-stoxx][@reuters-stoxx-close][@reuters-europe-week] Deutsche Bank fell 6% on Tuesday after media reports based on leaked documents detailed the bank’s dealings.[@reuters-europe-nov17] In Brussels the BEL 20 lost more than 100 points on Tuesday alone, closing at 5,986.98.[@lalibre-bel20-nov17] On Friday the bank KBC fell 3.35%, and AB InBev was the only BEL 20 share to rise.[@lalibre-bel20] In Asia, Taiwan’s market slid as investors unwound AI-related bets over concerns about valuations (how highly shares are priced), profitability and rising debt, and Japan’s Nikkei had its lowest close since April 13.[@reuters-taiwan][@reuters-nikkei] From Monday, Taiwan will fully ban short-selling below the previous day’s closing price.[@reuters-taiwan]

explain: **What is short-selling?** A short-seller borrows shares, sells them, and hopes to buy them back later at a lower price before returning them. The difference is their profit. Short-selling lets investors bet on falling prices, but in a fast fall it can add extra selling pressure, which is why some markets limit it during a rout.

**Crypto fell with tech.** Bitcoin lost 13.8% over the week.[@yahoo-btc] Reuters linked the slide in crypto markets to concern about the profits from massive AI spending, rising borrowing costs and regulatory risks following Democrats’ win in the US midterm elections.[@reuters-bitcoin]

## Other news that mattered

- **Anthropic’s record listing.** The AI company priced its shares at $83 and raised $81.3 billion, the largest stock market debut on record. The shares opened at $111.50 on Thursday, then fell 9.1% on Friday.[@ap-anthropic-ipo][@reuters-anthropic-ipo][@reuters-thu-anthropic-debut][@reuters-fri-correction]
- **Walmart beat forecasts and barely moved.** Its profit per share was 65 cents, just above the 64 cents analysts expected, and the shares fell a little less than 1% in afternoon trading.[@ap-walmart]
- **US shoppers kept spending.** Retail sales, what Americans spend in shops, rose 0.2% in October, in line with what economists expected.[@reuters-retail-sales]
- **UK inflation ticked up.** Prices in the UK were 3.6% higher than a year earlier in October, up from 3.5% the month before and in line with forecasts.[@ons-uk-cpi][@scmp-uk-cpi]
- **Japan’s economy shrank slightly.** It contracted at an annualized rate (the pace over a full year if it kept going) of 0.1% in July to September, hurt by surging oil prices because of the war in Iran.[@ap-japan-gdp]

explain: **What is an IPO?** An initial public offering (IPO) is when a company sells its shares on a stock exchange for the first time. Before it, the company is owned by its founders, staff and early investors; after it, anyone can buy a piece. The price is set the day before trading starts, and the first trades show how much demand there really is.
